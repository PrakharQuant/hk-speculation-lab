State-Dependent Expected Returns

Each investor expects the risk-free gross return in the state where they are the marginal buyer. In the other state they expect a lower return (and do not hold the asset).

Bubble Comparison

A. Fundamental-Value Pricing

Price equals the discounted expected dividends under the investor's own beliefs. No resale premium.

B. Harrison–Kreps Speculative Pricing

Price exceeds both investors' fundamental values because of the option to resell to a more optimistic investor when beliefs disagree and short sales are restricted.

C. Rational Bubble (Illustrative)

An additional component \( b_t \) satisfying \( b_t = \delta E[b_{t+1}] \) (no-arbitrage growth). Here we show a simple exploding example for contrast. Not part of the HK equilibrium.

A high price relative to fundamentals does not by itself prove a bubble exists in real markets.

Mathematical Research Notes

States \( \{0,1\} \), dividends \( d = [0,1]^\top \).

Agent 1 transition: \( Q_1 = \begin{pmatrix} \varphi & 1-\varphi \\ 1-\varphi & \varphi \end{pmatrix} \)

Agent 2 transition: \( Q_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1-\varphi & \varphi \\ \varphi & 1-\varphi \end{pmatrix} \)

Fundamental value: \( F^a = \delta Q_a (I - \delta Q_a)^{-1} d \)

Equilibrium: \( p = \delta \max_a E^a[d_{t+1} + p(d_{t+1}) \mid d_t] \)

Closed-form (symmetric case): \( p = \dfrac{\delta \varphi}{1-\delta} \)

Glossary

  • Heterogeneous beliefs: Agents disagree on transition probabilities.
  • Fundamental value: Discounted expected dividends under an agent's own beliefs, ignoring resale.
  • Speculative premium: \( p - F^a \). Value of the resale option.
  • Marginal buyer: The agent who is most optimistic in the current state and sets the price.
  • Short-selling: Restricted, so pessimists cannot force the price down to their valuation.
